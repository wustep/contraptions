# Nostos · after The Odyssey — research

What the show is built from: Christopher Nolan's *The Odyssey* (Universal, 17 July 2026; written and directed by Nolan;
the first feature shot entirely on IMAX 70 mm film; Matt Damon as Odysseus, Anne Hathaway as Penelope, Tom Holland as
Telemachus, Zendaya as Athena, Charlize Theron as Calypso, Samantha Morton as Circe, Robert Pattinson as Antinous;
cinematography Hoyte van Hoytema; score Ludwig Göransson). Compiled 19 October 2026 from reviews, interviews and
explainers. Claims resting on one secondhand source are marked *(uncertain)*. Sources are listed at the end.

## Nolan's reading

- **A reckoning, not an adventure.** Nolan: "His ultimate mistake is the horse." The horse is a gift turned into a weapon,
  so it breaks the guest-host code the film calls **Zeus's Law**. That gives the hero a tragic flaw from the first
  frame (TIME, July 2026). Critics call the film "Ancient Greek PTSD" (THR) and "Nolan's angriest film". Odysseus: "To
  burn the walls of Troy was to burn the world entire" (Slate).
- **The Sea Peoples are the Greeks.** Ithaca fears raiders "from the sea". The late turn is that the raiders were the
  Greeks themselves, coming home from Troy, and that this began the Bronze Age collapse. Nolan balances the pessimism
  with Penelope's line "Civilization will rise again" (TIME; Inverse; Emanuel Levy's Beijing interview).
- **Gods as weather.** An opening card reads "In a Time of Apparent Magic". Zeus appears only as thunder and Poseidon
  only as the sea. Athena (Zendaya) is the one god on screen, as a recurring vision, and her face turns out to be a
  Trojan priestess the Greeks killed. Nolan would not say whether she is real. The monsters are physical and built.
- **The man, not the myth.** The film calls itself "very grounded" (TIME). The flashbacks play as real events, and
  Odysseus is a veteran avoiding home rather than a trickster telling tales (Engelsberg; LRB).

## The story as Nolan tells it

- **Frame.** The film opens on a bard (Travis Scott) chanting the horse story in the suitors' hall. Its first image is
  the huge black horse listing half-buried in the surf. The first half hour is Ithaca *in medias res*. The main frame is
  Calypso's beach on Ogygia, where the lotus wears off and Odysseus recovers his voyage as memory (Guardian; Den of
  Geek). The horse is told three times: by the bard, by Menelaus to Telemachus, and by Odysseus to Penelope (The
  Ringer).
- **Order of the voyage.** Troy and the horse; the Cyclops; the Laestrygonians (two of three ships lost); Circe; Hades;
  the Sirens; Scylla and Charybdis; Thrinacia (Apollo's cattle, not Helios'); the storm; Ogygia, seven years; the raft;
  Ithaca.
- **Kept, changed and cut.**
  - **Aeolus and the bag of winds:** cut.
  - **The Lotus-eaters' island:** cut. The lotus moves to Calypso and causes his amnesia.
  - **The Cyclops:** kept, but the "Nobody" pun is cut. They blind him with a heated stake and escape among the sheep.
  - **Nausicaa and the Phaeacians:** cut.
  - **The olive-tree bed test:** cut. Recognition comes through a keepsake *(uncertain: a pin or an Athena figure)*.
  - **The hanging of the maids:** cut.
  - **Argos:** expanded. He recognises Odysseus, wags once and dies, and that is how Telemachus knows him.
  - **The Sirens:** kept. Odysseus is lashed to the mast, the crew have wax in their ears, we never hear the song, and
    one sailor tears out his wax and jumps.
  - **The bow and the axe heads:** kept. Penelope sets the trial, every suitor fails, and Odysseus strings the bow and
    plucks it. Its twang, played on a lyre in the score, is how Penelope knows him. The arrow goes through the row of
    axe heads.
  - **The ending:** changed. He gives the throne to Telemachus and sails west into exile with Penelope ("Not death.
    Exile."), to honour his unburied dead, "chasing the escaping sun". The last image is the two of them sailing into
    the sunset *(sources differ: one names the horse's eye in Troy's rubble as the last shot)*.
- **People.**
  - **Penelope (Hathaway).** "A volcano of a human that was always simmering." She is an active partner: she sets the
    bow test and hears the confession through a wooden screen. Hathaway trained on a real upright loom.
  - **Telemachus (Holland).** He is the only one kind to the beggar, fights at his father's side, and is crowned.
  - **Antinous (Pattinson).** He sent Sinon to die in his place, refuses to try the bow, runs, and is killed last.

## The film's look

- **Format and craft.** IMAX 65 mm in a 1.43:1 frame, graded photochemically. Practical first:
  - The horse is real: 36 ft, planked like a hull, with no wheels. It was dragged from the surf by hundreds of men.
  - The ship is a real Viking replica (*Draken Harald Hårfagre*) with a square sail, sailed on the real sea.
  - Polyphemus is a 60 ft animatronic with one vertical eye, modelled on Goya's *Saturn*.
  - The Laestrygonians are 7 ft stuntmen in chrome armour, shot in forced perspective.
- **Palette.** "Beige and green, with an occasional pop of blue or dull red" (A. E. Larsen); "burnt, bleached vistas of
  sand, scrub and sea" (Variety); a sea kept off its cliché colour (Bradshaw). Each place has its own identity, and
  the storms are dim with strobing lightning.
- **Places.**
  - **Troy** (Aït Benhaddou): red clay, a white Athena beheaded.
  - **The Cyclops' cave** (Nestor's Cave, Pylos).
  - **The Laestrygonians** (Culbin Forest): fog and pines.
  - **Hades** (Iceland's black sand under a hazy midnight sun): the dead massed on the shore, hands rising from the
    sand.
  - **The Sirens:** jagged rocks in surf.
  - **Ogygia** (Dakhla's white dune over a turquoise sea; Malta for the cave).
  - **Ithaca** (Favignana's clifftop fortress): the palace hall, petrol blue and deep red, "the colours of Ithaca".
- **Images critics singled out.**
  - The black horse listing in the surf, and the men hauling it ashore.
  - The night gate's gears turning.
  - The Cyclops' vertical eye.
  - The silver giants in foggy pines.
  - Hands rising from the black sand.
  - Odysseus lashed to the mast in silence.
  - A lone raft on a black sea.
  - The bowstring's pluck and the arrow through the axe heads.
  - The sail west into the sunset.

## The score, and the cue

- **Approach.** Nolan wanted no orchestra ("It's not like the orchestra existed back then"). Göransson built the score
  from:
  - 35 bronze gongs, close-miked and recorded with synthesizers;
  - a reconstructed aulos and lyre (Nolan wanted the lyre's pluck for the bow);
  - Albanian iso-polyphony and shepherds' flutes recorded in southern Albania;
  - the voices of James Blake and Travis Scott.

  The Odysseus theme is a thumping bass figure with a descending line that almost never resolves. The Ithaca motif is
  a descending figure on the aulos.
- **"Odysseus" (track 20 of 23, 8:41).** It most probably scores the final-act confession to Penelope, with the
  second, hellish sack of Troy as flashback *(Cineplay; consistent with the album's order: "Welcome Home Stranger" →
  "Odysseus" → "The Trial of the Bow / Vengeance" → "Chasing the Escaping Sun")*. Reviewers describe its shape:
  - **Opening:** aulos and a mournful lyre carry the Odysseus theme and the Ithaca motif.
  - **Middle:** a desolate stretch of bronze-gong clangs, then forceful percussion and chanting.
  - **End:** the Ithaca motif swells into "a real return rather than a fantasy".

  Movie Music UK calls it "by some margin the score's standout cue". The measured structure is in `cue.md`.
- **The upload.** Back Lot Music's own ("Provided to YouTube by Back Lot Music", ℗ 2026), video
  [WCxx_OVOVUw](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WCxx_OVOVUw), 8:41. The show plays it by YouTube only.

## What the show takes from this

- **A reckoning, told in order.** A wordless contraption cannot nest flashbacks, so the voyage goes forward. Nolan's
  structure stays in the frame: it opens on his first image, the black horse half-sunk in the surf. It cuts to Ithaca
  during the theme's second statement, as his film does in its first half hour. Every person who will be there at the
  end is set out there before he leaves Troy.
- **Locked cast.** People are balls; everything else is drawn. Key figures of the film are each one legible ball.
  - **Odysseus** is bronze, for the age that ends with him.
  - **His men** are clay. They are lost trial by trial, and the dead on the black sand are many like them.
  - **Penelope** is madder red, the colour of her thread.
  - **Telemachus** is paler bronze and smaller.
  - **The suitors** are slate; **Antinous**, who sent Sinon to die and will not try the bow, is darker and bigger.
  - **Calypso** is sea-glass: beside him on Ogygia through the seven years, and gone into her cave when he wakes.
  - **Agamemnon** is gold and bigger than any man, at the head of his host when Troy's gate goes up.
  - **The Trojans** are terracotta, Troy's own clay: they haul the horse in, cheering, and test it with their spears.
  - **Argos**, the horse, the Cyclops, the giants, the Sirens, the loom and the bow are drawn.
  - **No gods**, as in the film: Zeus is the bolt and Poseidon is the sea.
- **One job per prop.**
  - **The loom** keeps time. Penelope weaves by day and unpicks by night through the prologue and the seven years.
  - **The gate's gears** let the Greeks into Troy.
  - **The stake on its fulcrum** blinds the Cyclops.
  - **The rope and the block** bind him to the mast.
  - **The fishermen's hoist** brings him up Ithaca's cliff.
  - **The bow** is the trial: strung with Penelope's red thread and drawn by a crank only a drop from the terrace will
    move. Its note on the swell is the lyre's pluck, and the arrow goes through the twelve eyes.
- **Kept from Nolan's choices.** The prologue's horse: dragged ashore by cheering Trojans, spears driven into it among
  the men, the gate opened from inside and the army pouring in, and the Trojans cut down in their own streets. Three ships, two lost to giants in fog. The Cyclops' vertical eye. Hands rising from
  black sand and the dead charging. The unheard Sirens and the man who jumps. Apollo's cattle. The lotus and seven
  years. The raft given to the storm. Argos's one wag. The bow's pluck as recognition, with no bed test. Antinous killed
  first; then the suitors who come at him killed in a battle in the hall, Telemachus at his side; those who never fight
  give in. The exile west into the sun with Telemachus left behind to rule. Circe and Scylla are left out for clarity at this length.
