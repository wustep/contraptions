# Maker · after Dune: Part Two — research

What the show is built from: Denis Villeneuve's *Dune: Part Two* (Legendary / Warner Bros., 2024; written by Villeneuve
and Jon Spaihts after Frank Herbert's novel; cinematography Greig Fraser; music Hans Zimmer), with its first part's spice
harvester. Compiled 21 October 2026 from interviews, making-of pieces and the album. Claims resting on one secondhand
source are marked *(uncertain)*. Sources are listed at the end.

## The beats the show leans on

- **The first worm ride.** Paul plants a thumper, waits on a dune, and as the worm passes rides the collapsing dune down
  onto its flank, sets his maker hooks in a seam between its segments, and is carried up as the worm rolls the opened
  segment away from the sand; he climbs and stands. Chani, Stilgar and the Fremen watch from a distance. It took 44 days
  to shoot. A 90-foot section of worm was built as a set piece; a "Worm Unit" developed its movement, an ascender rig
  for the slide down the dune, and a gimballed platform of worm skin that rolled and tilted under Timothée Chalamet.
  Fraser modelled it on surfing and avalanche footage and kept the camera simple: steady on Paul as he waits, handheld
  on Chani's nerves.
- **Giedi Prime's arena.** The Harkonnen homeworld under a black sun: there is no visible light there, only infrared,
  so the sequence was shot on Alexas with their infrared filters removed (and native black-and-white Alexa 65s), in
  daylight. The skin comes out ghoul-pale and the Harkonnens hairless. Villeneuve: a gladiator scene in sand "will look
  like Arrakis", hence black and white; "the sunlight will kill colors". Costumes were tested fabric by fabric to see what
  read black or white. Feyd-Rautha fights three Atreides prisoners on his birthday, two of them drugged; the arena is a
  real set extended in CG.
- **The black fireworks.** The birthday's fireworks are "negative fireworks", black blooms in the white sky, made from
  drops of ink falling into clear alcohol (VFX producer Brice Parker's idea, after several others were tried).
- **The eclipse.** A real eclipse was filmed in the Jordanian desert for the film. The album has a cue called *Eclipse*.
- **The final charge.** Paul and the Fremen use the Atreides atomics and the worms to take Arrakeen: the worms come
  out of a sandstorm at the Emperor's camp, the Fremen riding them, against the Sardaukar and the Harkonnens. Critics
  differ on how much of the battle is shown; the climax is the duel after it.
- **The spice harvester (Part One).** A huge crawler on the open sand under a plume of spice; worm sign; a carryall that
  comes to lift it; the mouth coming up beneath it.

## The look

- **Colour as story.** Orange and white heat on Arrakis by day; the eclipse's dark; Giedi Prime in infrared black and
  white; colour back in the storm. The show takes this arc as its spine: the grade (`maker/grade.ts`) turns every colour
  in the frame from the desert's own, through an infrared red without hue as the moon covers the sun, to black and white
  on Giedi Prime, and back in the storm.
- **Scale.** Long lenses and tiny figures in vast frames; the sky often most of the frame; dunes as huge smooth forms
  with knife-edge crests, one side lit and one in shadow.
- **Restraint.** Few cuts in the big moments; long holds; sound and music carrying the scale.

## How the show uses it

| Film | The show |
| --- | --- |
| The harvester and the carryall (Part One) | The opening: worm sign on the booms, the carryall on the great blows, the mouth coming up round the machine. |
| The thumper and the first ride | The thumper beats with the drive; the ride is the run, the steadiest stretch of the cue. |
| The eclipse | The silence: the moon over the sun, the colour drained to infrared and then black and white. |
| The arena under the black sun | The two hardest blows of the cue; the third prisoner; the ink in the sky. |
| The charge out of the storm | The quickening beat: worms out of the storm, the Emperor's ship brought down. |
| Chani walking away at the end | Under the credits, small, going into the dunes. |
