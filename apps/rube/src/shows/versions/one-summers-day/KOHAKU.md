# Kohaku

`/shows/one-summers-day/opus55/` · **Kohaku**, one take, **Opus 5.5**, on the Movies shelf.

**Kohaku · after Spirited Away.** Joe Hisaishi's *One Summer's Day* is the opening theme of Hayao Miyazaki's *Spirited
Away* (Studio Ghibli, 2001); Hisaishi later set words to it as *The Name of Life*. The show plays his own recording with
the London Symphony Orchestra (*Melodyphony*, 2010) whole, by YouTube, from the official upload on his own channel
(TK1Ij_-mank), from its first sample, and the end credits run on into the quiet after it. No recording is shipped
(`ATTRIBUTION.txt`). The research is in `docs/shows/kohaku/research.md` and the cue's measured structure in
`docs/shows/kohaku/cue.md`.

*Kohaku* is the river Haku was, and the name she gives back to him.

## The idea

The film turns on a name. A girl crosses into the spirit world at dusk, works in its bathhouse, sees the boy who helped
her come in over the sea as a white dragon torn by paper birds, goes out over the flooded plain on the train to save
him, and coming home on his back remembers the river she fell into when she was small, and says its name, which is his.
His scales come off him and they fall together. In the morning she goes home and does not look back.

Four minutes of music will not hold the whole film. The show keeps that spine and the images the film is remembered
by, each given room: the gate and the wind, the meadow and its cloud, the river of stones, the lanterns coming on, the
bathhouse lit at night, the river spirit, the new sea at dawn, the dragon and the paper birds, the train over the water,
the hopping lamp, the flight under the moon, the scales, the fall, and the arch.

The cue is shaped like a short film: four soft chords, the theme alone, two climbs to the same cadence, a deep quiet,
and the theme a last time to the fullest climax before a long fall away. The story is laid on it: the arrival on the
quiet, the bathhouse on the first climb, the dragon on the second, the train on the quiet, the name on the last cadence,
the way home on the fall.

**The cast is locked.** Everyone who is a person is a ball:
- Chihiro is a rose ball, the pink of her work clothes at the bathhouse.
- Haku is a pale jade-white ball, his dragon's white with the green of his mane in its rim: a boy on the bridge, the
  dragon over the sea and under the moon, a boy again when his name comes back to him.
- No-Face is a dark ball, bigger than she is, his white mask on him: on the bridge as she crosses, and beside her on the
  train.
- The bathhouse's hands are cream balls, who haul on the rope with her.

Everything else is drawn: the river spirit, Kamaji's arms and the soot, the dragon, the paper birds, the shadow
passengers and the girl on the platform, the lamp.

**One job per prop:**
- The tunnel lets her in and out.
- The lanterns tell the dusk.
- The token sends for the water.
- The rope pulls the thorn.
- The shutter shuts out the birds.
- The train carries her over the water.
- The lamp lights the way to the cottage.
- The river under the cloud gives back his name.

## How it is made

`one-summers-day/kohaku/`:
- `show.ts` is one world on one clock (`KohakuShow`): the balls of the cast, each on a path of its own. The stage draws
  what the show lists (`ShowPoint.balls`), so Chihiro's mark turns only as she rolls (`path.ts`: rests, rolls, hops
  under one gravity, glides, rides).
- `scene.ts` and `scenes/` lay the film out as scenes, each owning a window of show time. A scene says where she is,
  who else is there, where the camera looks and what is drawn; `score.ts` lays them end to end.
- `camera.ts` is an authored director: monotone cubics through holds and follows, the follow never averaging across a
  cut; cuts only on the music; dissolves for changes of place and time.
- `light.ts` is one keyed plan of the light for the whole show, the film's day twice over (an afternoon into dusk, the
  bathhouse's night, a dawn and a day, the train's long evening into night, the moon over the cloud, the morning home),
  which the sky and every set read.
- `world/` is the sky (flat cumulus in two tones, a field of them over the whole world), the sea of cloud and its gap,
  and the dragon (`dragon.ts`: a spine that is his head's own way behind it, a mane, horns and legs, and the burst).
- `geo.ts` is the world's lie, west to east: the gate, the meadow, the river of stones, the town, the bridge, the
  bathhouse, the sea and its stations.
- `scenes/` holds the six scenes and their sets: `way/` (the gate, the meadow, the river, the steps, the town),
  `bathhouse/` (the building drawn whole with its fronts taken away where a scene goes in, `building.ts` and `set.ts`;
  the bridge; the great bath's hall, the river spirit, the boiler room, No-Face's mask), `dragon/` (his way in, the
  paper birds, her room), `train/` (the line, the car inside and out, the stations, Swamp Bottom and the lamp), and
  `sky.ts`, the flight and the fall shared by the name and home. `world/sea.ts` is the water under every eastern scene.

The look is flat fills and few ink lines. Ink weights scale with the camera, like the balls' own outlines. Depth comes
from layers paling into the air, and light only from where it comes from, as flat shapes on what it falls on. There is
no glow, bloom or sparkle, and nothing is written on the frame. The end credits are the page's (`credits.ts`), painted
into the frame only in a saved video.

## The clock

The cue is played freely, with no click, so `scripts/shows/kohaku-onsets.py` measures its stretches and landmarks,
every onset with its strength, and the eighths tracked through its rubato, into `scripts/shows/plans/kohaku-onsets.json`.
`apps/rube/checks/kohaku.ts` holds:
- every strike the story turns on to that file (the chords at the gate, the stones going in and going home, the steps,
  the lanterns, the dragon at the door, the shutter, the birds on it, his blows, his name, the parting);
- every bare camera cut to it;
- every ball's way whole, and nobody jumping, coming or going in shot but at a cut or across a dissolve;
- the credits and the soundtrack to what they owe.

## The rounds

*A log, in the order the rounds were made: what each looked at, what it found and what it changed.*

**Round 0, the first build.** The kit, the way in, the dragon, the name and home were built in the director's
worktree. The bathhouse's night and the train were built in parallel worktrees from the same kit and integrated by
copying their scene folders. The sea is the train builder's (`world/sea.ts`) and lies under every eastern scene.

**Round 1, at the viewer's size.** The whole show was played in real time, silent, in six parallel 46 s chunks on a
1440×810 desktop page and screenshotted twice a second, and the key beats were seen on a 390×844 phone held upright.
A frame-to-frame sweep at 30 fps flagged only the intended cuts.
- **The gate.** For its first ten seconds she was a dot in a wall of red. The camera now sits closer on the arch as
  she comes up out of it and goes out into the sun, then opens on the summer.
- **The bridge.** Haku came to her inside the reveal's pull-back, two specks. The meeting is now seen at the bridge's
  foot, the bathhouse's lit footings at the frame's edge, and the camera goes up and back to the whole building only as
  they set off across together.
- **The crest.** The haul held one medium shot for ten seconds. The camera now closes in pull by pull, and opens as
  the thorn comes free to take the junk pouring out.
- **The river spirit's going.** Outside, the ribbon of him came a second late and read as the chimney's smoke. It is
  paler and fuller, the old face at its head, and already rising over the roofs at the cut.
- **The boiler room.** The soot carrying the coal was half off the frame's edge; the shot holds a little further right.
- **The sea's wide.** It was empty for five seconds while the dragon was still out of frame. It is centred further
  out, and he comes in at its edge.
- **The car.** Its last camera key before the dusk held still while the car went on, so it slid in the frame for two
  frames before the dissolve; the camera is keyed to the car to the cut.
- **The far houses on the sea** stood in an even row like markers; they are fewer, and uneven.
- **Over the cloud,** on a phone the meadow's far hills showed as a green band over the cloud sea. They are not drawn
  from that high.

**Round 2, the camera and the bath.** The camera's pan and zoom were measured every sixtieth of a second over the whole
show, in frame heights and in log-size a second, and every window over a threshold was looked at.
- **Into the hall** the camera dived from the bathhouse's wide to the door and swung through the front into the hall at
  nearly two frame heights a second. The wide now holds while they cross, and on the note she reaches the door the
  picture cuts in to her at it, the hall's front going away as she goes in.
- **Before the name** it closed from the gap's wide to the two of them at over one log-unit a second; it now starts
  sooner and arrives on the cadence. The pull-backs off the balcony, before the sea's wide and as the dragon wheels in,
  are a second longer each, and the landing's push-in comes a little after they touch down.
- **The great bath** read murky: dark wood, a row of night windows high in the back wall, a brown room. The wall is
  the warm wood of a lamplit room, the windows are cut, and once the hot water runs a little steam lifts off it, few
  and slow.
- **The dragon's head** was a plain white shape. It now has a sea-green eye under a brow, an ear's fin edged with
  the mane's green, two forked pale horns, and a muzzle that lifts a little at the nose.
- The whole show was played again in real time on a 390×844 phone held upright. In the band where the 16:9 frame
  sits, every beat reads.

**Round 3, the looks and the name.** The cast had no way to look at one another: a ball's mark turned only as it
rolled, so every meeting and parting was carried by motion alone.
- **The looks.** While a ball is still or carried its mark now turns to what it looks at and back, eased over a little
  under half a second (`Look` in `show.ts`); looks that overlap hand over without falling back between, and
  `check:shows` holds every look to a ball that is not rolling. Haku watches her from the bridge, and she him as he
  comes. She watches the sea at dawn, the dragon all the way in over it, and him on the floor. At Swamp Bottom she
  watches the lamp hop to her, and the dragon come down. Over the cloud she looks down at the river in the gap, then
  at him as his name comes back. Falling, and on the stones, they face each other. When she goes, he watches her go
  all the way to the gate; she does not look back.
- **The name** was the show's climax staged small: the camera tight on her, the scales starting off-frame at his tail,
  a quick scatter of thin flakes gone in a second. The cadence now frames the whole of him; the white comes off him in
  a wave from the tail to her over two seconds, the scales larger, drifting back and up over the place and lingering
  five seconds; and the fall is framed wider, the two of them going down away from them.
- **The credits** were checked as a saved video paints them, in a 1920×1080 frame and in the 9:16 letterbox: they sit
  in the sky where the page sets them.

**Round 4, the sets that were unfinished.** The whole show was swept again every three seconds at a small size, looking
for the frames that read as unfinished or busy beside the rest.
- **The river of stones** was a flat beige band with a row of small ovals. It now has a few boulders lying in it at
  their own sizes, a pale gravel top, a darker near edge, and grass where it meets its banks.
- **The far bank** was a bare pyramid of stone over a brown slab. It is now a green bank with the steps set into it as
  stone slabs, a bush at their foot, and moss along the top of the town's wall.
- **The great bath's gallery** was a fence of small red balusters over the whole back wall; it is two rails on a few
  posts. The floor's board joints are half as many.
- **The gorge** under the bridge was a grey slab lighter than the night over it. It now goes down into the dark, the
  piers and the town's bank with it.
- **No-Face** hovered over the bridge's deck like something hung there. He sits on its rail, quite still, as they
  pass under him.

**Round 5, the name.** The whole show was played again in real time on a 1440×810 desktop page and a 390×844 phone,
twice a second, with fresh eyes. The last climax was the weakest stretch.
- **The river in the gap** was a dark oval with a thin pale curve at its foot, mostly behind the cloud's near rim: on a
  phone, a hole. The country in it is now moonlit green, hazing as it goes away, and the river winds silver through
  the middle of it, widening towards us.
- **The gap was in frame for about a second.** The camera rose with him and left it below. He now comes down nearer
  the cloud as the gap opens, the camera holds it while he flies in over it, and the cadence finds the whole of him
  over his river.
- **The scales** were thin white flicks, left behind in one frame, gone off the frame's edge in a second: they read
  as hairlines, and on a phone as nothing. They are now fewer and larger, round-backed shells each about his body's
  width, and each goes on with him at his speed when it comes off, slowed by the air, so he draws away from it.
  Remembering where each came away also halved the burst's frame time.
- **The fall** was two specks in a wide sky. The camera now closes on the two of them as the last of the dragon goes,
  and holds them close down into the cloud.
- **The landing:** round 4's bush at the steps' foot stood just where they come down, and Haku sat in it like an eye
  in a shrub. The bush is cut.

**Round 6, her room and the lamp.** The dragon's twelve seconds on her floor, after the shutter, played as a still
picture, and the lamp at Swamp Bottom was hard to tell from the line's posts.
- **On her floor** his legs went down through the boards into the storey below, and the writhe of his blows was a
  few pixels. Lying, his legs now fold back along his flanks and he lies on his belly on the floor. On each blow his
  head comes up off the floor and his back heaves and they go down again, each blow weaker, until he is still.
- **The shutter** dropped and nothing changed in the room; a soft glow lay on the floor under her as if the day still
  came in. The glow is cut, and when the shutter drops the day is shut out: the room and all in it go a shade dimmer,
  and she goes to his head in the dim.
- **The lamp** hopped beside the line's posts, the same dark height with a crossarm, and read as one more of them. The
  line sets no posts on Swamp Bottom's island now (the wire goes over the wood to the next one), so the lamp is the
  only post there.

## Known limits

- **The bathhouse is long.** The great bath holds the stage from 54 s to 94 s, broken only by the boiler room and the
  shot outside; every beat in it is on the music, and it was kept whole because the crest needs its build.
- **The fall through the cloud** goes to the cloud's pale for about a second; it is a slow change of the whole frame,
  measured at one swing, not a flash.
- **Inside the car** the line's posts pass the windows about once a second, so a frame-to-frame sweep sees steady
  change there; it is the train going, and was kept.
- **The flight's dragon is small** on a phone held upright, where the 16:9 frame is a band across the middle of a tall
  sky; the cadence frames the whole of him so the burst still reads there.
- **YouTube's player** decides when the music starts; the show's clock follows it (the shared `youtube.ts`), as every
  YouTube show's does.
