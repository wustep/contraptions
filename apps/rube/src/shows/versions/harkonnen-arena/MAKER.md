# Maker

`/shows/harkonnen-arena/opus55/` · **Maker**, one take, **Opus 5.5**, on the Movies shelf.

**Maker · after Dune: Part Two.** Hans Zimmer's *Harkonnen Arena* is from *Dune: Part Two (Original Motion Picture
Soundtrack)* (WaterTower Music, 2024). In Denis Villeneuve's film it is the cue of chant and drums under Feyd-Rautha's
birthday games on Giedi Prime. Here it is played whole by YouTube from WaterTower Music's own upload (UdlvgIFdNZU), from
its first sample, and then the end credits run on into the quiet after it: 347 s in all. No recording is shipped
(`ATTRIBUTION.txt`). Why this cue, and its measured structure, are in `docs/shows/maker/cue.md`; the research behind the
beats is in `docs/shows/maker/research.md`.

The Fremen call the sandworm Shai-Hulud, and the Maker: the hooks a rider sets in its seams are maker hooks.

## The idea

Part Two tells its story in light. Arrakis by day is orange and white heat; an eclipse takes the sun; Giedi Prime, where
there is no visible light, only infrared, is black and white; and the colour comes back with the storm the Fremen ride
out of. The show takes that arc as its spine. Every colour in every frame goes through one grade (`maker/grade.ts`):
the desert's own colours, then, as the moon covers the sun in the cue's silence, an infrared red without hue, then black
and white on Giedi Prime, and the colour given back on the storm's dissolve.

Its beats are the film's: a harvester taken in the deep desert (from Part One), the thumper and the first worm ride, the
arena under the black sun, and the charge out of the storm. The cue's shape holds them: its first stretch of booms and
great blows is the harvester's end, its two driving pulses are the thumper and the ride, and the charge, its dead
silence is the eclipse, and its two hardest blows land in the arena.

**The cast is locked.** Everyone who is a person is a ball:
- Paul is a blue ball, the blue within blue of the spice's eyes.
- Chani is madder red. She watches the ride from the mesa, and rides beside him in the charge.
- The Fremen are dun, the colour of their cloaks in the sand.
- Feyd-Rautha is chalk white, a little bigger, only ever on Giedi Prime.
- The three Atreides prisoners in his arena are grey.
- The Harkonnen crowd in the arena's tiers and the Emperor's Sardaukar are drawn as masses, not as balls.

Everything else is drawn: the harvester, the carryall, the worms, the thumper, the arena, the Emperor's ship.

**One job per prop:**
- The rock keeps the two of them safe: the worm cannot come up through stone.
- The harvester is the Harkonnen machine the desert takes; the carryall comes for it and cannot save it.
- The thumper calls the worm: it beats with the drum.
- The maker hooks open a seam, so the worm rolls the open side up and carries him.
- The moon takes the sun, and the colour with it.
- Feyd's knife.
- The black fireworks are the Harkonnens' birthday.
- The storm hides the worms; the Emperor's ship falls.

## How it is made

`harkonnen-arena/maker/`:
- `show.ts` is one world on one clock (`MakerShow`): the balls of the cast, each on a path of its own (`motion.ts`:
  holds, rolls over the sand, true-gravity hops, rides). The stage draws what the show lists (`ShowPoint.balls`), and
  each dot turns only as its ball rolls, or turns to look.
- `scene.ts` and `timeline.ts` lay the film out as scenes, each owning a window of show time: where Paul and Chani are,
  who else is on the stage, the camera's shots, what is drawn, its strikes. `score.ts` lays them end to end.
- `lens.ts` is an authored camera: monotone cubics through holds and tracks; cuts only on the music; dissolves at the
  changes of place.
- `grade.ts` is the arc of light, over every colour a drawing asks for (`paint.ts`: `b.c(hex)`).
- `world/` is the desert: the sky and the sun, ranges of dunes with knife-edge crests and slip faces in shadow, drawn in
  layers as through a dolly (a far range is `h / (h + d)` of its size, so close shots keep a low horizon), the worm's
  skin and mouth.
- `scenes/` holds the places: `desert/` (the harvester and the maw), `ride/` (the thumper, the ridden worm, the eclipse),
  `arena/` (Giedi Prime), `charge/` (the storm, the camp, the worms, the evening).

The look is flat fills and few lines. Lines never fall under 1.25 CSS pixels. Depth comes from layers paling toward the
air; light only from where it comes from, as flat shapes on what it falls on. There is no glow, bloom or sparkle, and
nothing is written on the frame. The end credits are the page's (`credits.ts`), painted into the frame only in a saved
video; on a phone they keep the house floor (`least` 4.5) and rise into the sky over the frame (`lift` 0.6).

## The clock

`scripts/shows/harkonnen-arena-onsets.py` measures the cue once into `scripts/shows/plans/harkonnen-arena-onsets.json`:
its stretches and landmarks, every onset with its strength, and five combs tracked beat by beat (the booms, the drum
under the great blows, the drive, the run, the charge). `apps/rube/checks/maker.ts` holds:
- every strike the story turns on to that file (each scene's `strikes`), and every camera cut;
- the arc of light to its stretches;
- every ball to never jumping, coming or going in shot, but across a cut or a dissolve;
- the credits and the soundtrack to what they owe.

## How it was made

The kit (the clock, the grade, paths, the camera, the dunes, the worm's skin) and the deep desert were the director's;
the ride, the arena and the charge were built in parallel by three builders in their own worktrees to a contract (each
scene's window, its seams, the cast it owns, the look's rules), and integrated by copying their folders. Each round
after the first build was a real-speed watch of the whole show at 1440 × 810 and on a phone held upright, with notes back
to the builders, who kept their context; each weighed cutting as much as adding.

- **First build.** All four scenes end to end on the music.
- **Round 1.** The opening draws back from the two of them on the rock's lip; the machine's close cranes up from its
  scoop to its plume; worm sign is a held wide the swell runs across; the swallow is seen close. The stand became the
  ride's longest shot; Paul is tracked and legible; worm sign in the ride is ground moving, not a curl. The arena gained
  four viewpoints (it repeated three framings), a crowd of five deep tiers that rocks as one mass (it was nine stripes),
  and ink that blooms and droops in fine threads (it was a four-armed pinwheel). The charge gained a value structure, a
  close on the riders, and thirty Fremen running out of the storm. Cut: the curl, the cheer after the stand, the
  frame-fixed day sun, the striped medium shots, the ink's arms.
- **Round 2.** The last image (it was a pale haze with two specks): Paul on a crest, Chani going away down the slope
  toward the sun. The eclipse's silence stays readable (it was a near-black screen for eight seconds), and its draw back
  takes eight seconds (it went tenfold in four). The open seam under the stand is a dark slit (it read as a flame). The
  bowl's shadow lies across the arena floor in the shots from the rim (they were plain grey). Fixed: a step in the sky
  where the stand now runs on into the draw back.
- **Round 3.** Subtraction: the storm's scalloped billows (a pattern) became a few great lobes; the worms' plates and
  seams went quieter; the dust at the strike no longer lies over the mouth as a halo; the resting Fremen gather in knots
  (an even row read as a dotted line); far Sardaukar ranks are one band.
- **Round 4.** Worm sign is held still, low, so the swell is seen to travel across a desert that does not move (the
  camera rode with it, and it read as one more dune). Cut: the grand wide of the bowl cut for a second and a half into the
  close shot of the ram, which read as the close shot interrupted.
- **Round 5.** The swallow, the cue's first climax and the film's first image of the worm: the mouth was a squat ring
  on the sand, still for seven seconds. It stands up out of a heap of sand, leaning, the body bending away east under the
  flat the way it came in, sand pouring off its lip; the machine is carried up in it and goes down its throat a step on
  every other beat of the drum; it goes straight back down on the fifth blow. The sand going down round the machine is a
  slow push in (it was a held wide). The share card is the machine in the risen mouth.
- **Round 6.** The charge. The storm read as a cliff: a crisp top, a flat lit band along each crest like strata, the back
  walls darkest like rock going into shade, and a dune range painted over its foot as two light ledges. The bands are
  cut; each wall is dense at its foot and thins into the sky, the further ones paler; what is behind it is hidden in it.
  The rear on the peak rose as a forty-degree ramp beside a ship wider than its mouth, and read as a hose: the neck now
  stands, curving up from its foot, twice the ship's height, the head flaring as it opens and curling over so the mouth
  looks down on a great ship smaller than it.
- **Round 7.** The people, where they were too small or too still to read. Paul was a speck at the foot of the mesa as
  he walked out to plant the thumper, and on the dune's slope as he climbed it: the walk pushes in to him as he leaves
  the mesa, and the climb goes up with him. The thirty Fremen on foot were dots along the horizon of a wide shot (two
  pixels on a phone): the camera runs low with the crowd, a stride a beat, the storm's foot behind them. After the
  strike, the close on the riders pushes in to Paul and Chani side by side, then opens, held, as they get down.
- **Round 8.** Feyd-Rautha's entrance, and the ride's worm sign. He and the prisoners came out of their gates as specks
  in the bowl's wide: on his gate's onset the camera cuts close, the door going up, and he steps out of the black pylon
  into the white and walks out under the black sun, the crowd a band low behind; then the three are seen, shoved in
  across the floor in a file. The ride's worm sign had the fault the desert's had before round 4 (the camera went along
  with the swell, so it stood still on screen): it is held low and nearly still, and the swell runs across the frame.
- **Round 9.** A whole-show watch after round 8. On a phone held upright, which shows more sky, the storm still showed
  its tops (a flat top and three stepped bands, a mesa again): its walls now tower out of any frame, fade out well below
  their crests, and are near one value. The carryall's hold was thirteen still seconds (the strain on each beat a
  hundredth of the frame): it now tugs on each beat of the drum, lifting the machine, and the camera pushes in.

Checked each round: `npm run build` (lint, typecheck, every check suite, the site build); a frame-difference sweep of
the whole show at 30 fps, whose only flags are the camera's cuts and the worm's strike on the great ship; every frame
painted at 1600 × 900 in about 0.4 ms (median), 0.7 ms (95th percentile), 4.6 ms at worst (the dissolve into the storm);
the camera's pan and zoom every 1/60 s; a saved video's credits at 1080p and in the Shorts letterbox.
