# Mountain King, Opus 5.5 (B)

Open it at `/shows/mountain-king/opus55-spark/` or `/shows/?show=mountain-king&take=opus55-spark`. In the Shows picker it is **Opus 5.5 (B)**, a take of the work **Mountain King**. Directed by Claude Opus 5.5.

## What it is

A Rube Goldberg machine plays Grieg's *In the Hall of the Mountain King*, whole, then runs its credits in silence. 179 s in all.

A candle's flame slips off its wick while the cat sleeps by the stove. It knows that every fire is a door. It goes into the stove and comes out of a glassworks' glory hole, rides the glassmaking out through the furnace into a balloon regatta at sunset, climbs the balloons and drops out of the top burner into the smokestack of a night express loaded with fireworks. The train runs away with it, faster and faster with the music, to the festival at the end of the line, where it sets off the finale. In the silence after, it lies in the ash all but out. On the roll it flares and dashes home back through every fire, lands on its wick on the first of the two last chords, and on the second the stove door bangs shut behind it. The cat's eye snaps open. It looks up at the candle, which is burning the way candles do, and goes back to sleep.

The brief asked for something with no mountain, cave, mine, troll, hall or Peer Gynt in it, for new worlds joined by portals, and for new mechanics. Here the portals are fires, which gives every world a reason to exist (it has a fire), a colour for its door, and a motivated match cut (into one flame, out of another). The music is mischief: a tiptoe, a runaway, a crash, a held breath, two blows. A small careful thing that sneaks out for the night and gets home by the last chord has that shape, and a stranger can read it from pictures.

**The cast.**
- **Spark**, the only ball (`#F2A33A`). Its heart and its flame are drawn in `spark/fx.ts`, not by the stage. The flame grows with the orchestra, from a candle's flicker in the loft to a comet on the express. In the silence its heart dulls under a crust of ash and its flame shrinks to a guttering tongue that never quite goes out, and on the first last chord it is a candle's flame again.
- **The cat**, a grey tabby, drawn and never a ball. It sleeps by the stove the whole show. It half wakes and listens when the drying rack's candles knock, twitches its tail when the reel bumps it, stretches in its sleep, and wakes on the last chord.

## The worlds, in order

Each world has its own palette, materials and gravity (`spark/worlds.ts`, `spark/physics.ts`), and each is a hotter, faster kind of fire than the last. None is one of Machine's worlds or any other show's.

## The doors

A door is a match cut on the spark. The part going out closes in until its fire fills the frame round the spark, and the part coming in opens on its own fire at the same framing and pulls out. The camera carries the framing across the cut, so the spark holds its place on the screen while the world round it changes (`spark/show.ts` shifts the camera by exactly the jump in cells). Over the cut `fx.ts` makes each door an opening the camera flies through with the spark. In the last quarter second the old fire's mouth (the stove's doorway, the furnace's arched port, the top burner's jet) grows about the point the spark goes in by until its iron and brick jambs pass the frame's edges; for two frames either side of the cut the frame is all fire; then the new world's mouth (the glory hole's arch, a burner's jet, the chimney) starts past the frame and closes to its true place as the camera backs out of it. The mouths are in `spark/mouths.ts`. Inside a mouth the fire is a passage: its licks fly out from a point just ahead of the spark and grow as they come, in three depths, so it streams back past the spark, and the spark stays in front with the fire going deeper round its flame (never a dark disc). Through the cut its heart stays round and trails a short smear the way it came. The fire is the old world's colours before the cut and the new world's after (`FIRES`): the stove's ember red, the furnace's white-gold, the burner's blue and gold, the engine's coal orange, going white-hot at the cut.

The three doors out are on phrase starts: 58.024 (statement 2), 82.053 (phrase 9) and 101.951 (statement 3). The three doors home are on the roll's strokes (148.330, 148.561, 148.817), with only a flash of fire at each cut, so the regatta and the glassworks are each seen for a quarter of a second as the spark comes out of their fires. The fire at a door is always one world's or the other's, never a mix: the old world's comes in, the new world's follows it, and where they meet it is white-hot (blue and orange mixed make grey).

## The music

- **The recording.** The Czech National Symphony Orchestra's public-domain Musopen recording, 154.091 s, shipped whole in `grieg-mountain-king-musopen.mp3`. The soundtrack also plays the orchestra's label upload from YouTube (`k8HCJS4FflY`, the same recording sample for sample, offset 0); the file is the fallback and what export uses. `ATTRIBUTION.txt` covers the recording and is shared with the other take of this work, so it names that take's file and story. Spark's story is its own.
- **The clock.** `scripts/shows/mountain-king-onsets.py` measured the recording once into `scripts/shows/plans/mountain-king-onsets.json`, shared with the other take and never edited here. It holds every quarter note of the theme (the accelerando from 104 to 198 bpm has no steady comb, so every quarter was tracked), the eighteen phrases (A A B B A A three times), the coda's 23 chords, the silence at 147.0, the roll at 148.243 and the two last chords at 149.515 and 149.815. `spark/music.ts` reads it: `beat(k)` is quarter k's show time, `phrase(n)` phrase n's first note.
- **What strikes.** In statements 1 and 2 the strong notes are bar 2's two figures and the downbeat. In statement 3 the whole band hits the backbeats, so the express and the festival strike those. The coda strikes its heavy chords. 186 strikes in all, every one on a measured onset or an eighth of the tracked beat.

## Craft notes

- **The loft is lit by the spark.** Once the candle goes out, everything near the spark is lit and everything far from it is in moonlight or dark (`sparkIn` in `fx.ts`). That is how the loft reads as a loft at night, and how the spark reads as the light. The floor is a section of the house: under the boards the joists stand in the dark over the lath-and-plaster ceiling of the room below, and wherever the loft is lit its light leaks down through the gaps.
- **The stage draws no ball.** `SparkShow.at` hands the stage an empty list of balls, and `fx.ts` draws the spark's heart as the first thing in each world's `over` pass. So it is a flame's heart with a soft gradient: no ink ring, no spinning dot, and no trail of beads behind it on the train. Pan lips and sockets drawn over it still cover it.
- **The flame leans back from the motion.** It is sampled from the spark's velocity over the last 40 ms, and it grows with the recording's loudness (`heat`).
- **The express keeps time with its wheels.** The wheels' turn is locked to `beat(k)`, so a quarter of a turn is always a quarter note however fast the music gets. On the engine the camera rides with the train, not with the spark (a key a quarter on one point of the engine), so the crank goes round inside a steady picture while the line runs past. On the last run it does the opposite: from riding at the train's speed it eases down to chasing it at about two thirds of its speed (the travel integrated, so the engine only ever gains), and the train outruns the camera.
- **The camera never whips; it cuts.** Four times the camera cuts inside a world, each on an eighth of the theme (`CAMERA_CUTS` in `music.ts`, `Shot.cut` in `camera.ts`, after Merry-Go-Round): to the cat and back for the knocks (the spark and the cat are 21 cells apart, and a two-shot of both made each a few pixels), and into and out of the express's chase, where a continuous camera had to overtake the train (the engine sliding back through the frame) and then whip on to the firebox. A cut changes the size by a real step, so none reads as a jump cut. It interrupts a move rather than braking it: the key before a cut keeps its speed into it, and the move after starts at its own, so a camera riding the engine is at the train's speed from the first frame.
- **The camera takes the hits in the body.** On the doors, the slam, the crash, the heaviest coda chords, the hammer blows and the two last chords it jolts in and eases back (`score.ts`). Where it is already drawing back through a hit (the crash, the Titan's burst, the first last chord) the jolt throws it further back instead, so the hit lands as a blow and not as a hitch in the move.
- **Hot glass reads as glass.** The gather is dark cherry glass with a hot core seen through it, longer along the pipe than it is tall, with a neck and a collar of glass round the iron; its folds and the window's streak turn with the pipe, and it has no ink outline and no round highlight. It droops into the pendant the mould takes and hands over to the hot bottle behind the shut mould. The spark on its crown stands apart from it on a soft contact shadow.
- **A fire is licks, not teardrops.** Every fire in the show (the stove, the glory hole, the furnace's port, the veil) is drawn by `spark/fire.ts`: licks on sinuous centrelines that fork into two or three tips, sized on a power law and rooted off any row, graded from white-gold to the rim and laid on additively so they burn white where they cross, with now and then a wisp breaking off. The spark's own flame is the only clean teardrop in the show.
- **The spark is findable in every frame.** Its heart keeps its true size (every socket holds), but its flame never draws smaller than about 6% of the frame's 16:9 band (`flameBoost`), so it is a few pixels tall even on a phone held upright in the widest shots; by day a warm light is added round it over the sunset, and among the festival's bursts a soft dusk round it lifts it off the gold. A candle burning on its wick is left a candle.
- **The leap home settles.** It is a curve on an easing clock, quick out of the fire and slowing onto the wick, not a thrown ball's parabola, which in the loft's gravity would take a second or land still rising.
- **The moon recedes as the fire takes the night.** It is laid out on the screen, not in the world. Off the smokestack it rises into its place as the camera pulls out, instead of standing beside the close-up spark. From the festival on it dims, and on the coda's first chord the mines' smoke rolls over it: by 135.4 it is all but gone, and it stays gone through the silence (`railway/night.ts`, `moonLight`). Until then every shell is placed clear of it; after, the finale has the whole sky.
- **The finale lights the night, and it stays a night.** Each burst lays its colour on the sky round it, lights the smoke from within, stands in the river as a broken column and catches the sky-facing edges of the guns, the Titan and the crate (`fireworks-draw.ts`, `skyGlow`). The glow is pooled round the bursts and kept off the far plain, so the corners stay navy and the land stays dark (gold laid over navy turns to khaki), and all of it goes out in 70 ms on the silence.
- **Machines in the quiet stretches.** The two stretches of statement 1 and 2 that were a ball touring props are machines now: the reel of wick the spark rides across the loft's floor (`loft/stove-spool.ts`), and the ringing carriage in the glassworks (`glass/glass-plan.ts`, `drawCarriage`), whose bottles stand where its hammer comes down on each note, spaced wider as it speeds up.
- **Nothing ball-sized near the hero.** The glory hole is an arched mouth on a sill, not a round port. The express's smoke and steam roll as soft billows with no disc edges: the chimney's cough, the plume, the whistle, and the cylinder steam, which lies low under the axles as haze the train pulls out of. The balloons' crowns are domes with a small sewn valve, not lids. Firework bursts open on a warm point-flash and are streaks and falling trails; crackle and glitter are soft glints, never squares or beads.
- **The ending waits for the cat.** The stove door hangs open while the spark lands on its wick and swings late, slamming on the second last chord. The credits start at 154.2, after the cat has looked and gone back to sleep. They sit in the dark over the roof, and the camera frames the whole loft so the candle is below them with all its light, the floor standing over the frame's foot on its joists, and holds that frame to the end. On a phone held upright they lift most of the way into the night sky over the roof (the house's `lift` on a title card, 0.7).
- **The spark in the balloons' silk is a flame behind a lampshade.** Riding up inside an envelope it is the same heart and flame it is outside, at the same size, and it lights the gores round it; a line of light runs up the hot air to it from the roaring burner; the camera tilts up after it a little behind, so the whole balloon is in the frame.
- **Known weaknesses.** Some of the coda's big battery shells reach past the frame's top at their widest (the three heaviest chords' shells break whole). Under Zoom the credits' Music card's two lines of fine print sit over the wall shelf and its jug (the 16:9 frame keeps the shelf under the cards; the words stay legible over the dim shelf), and the Titan mortar slides out at the left edge as the camera pushes in on the salutes.

## How it is built

- `mountain-king/opus55-spark.show.ts`: the version file. Everything with weight is behind `load()`.
- `mountain-king/spark/`: the director's files. `score.ts` (the order of legs and parts, the sets, the camera per leg carried across each door, the punches), `show.ts` (`SparkShow`, one ball through four worlds), `kit.ts` and `camera.ts` (the part contract and the director, after Liftoff and All at Once), `music.ts`, `seams.ts` (every seam's velocity and camera distance), `physics.ts`, `worlds.ts`, `fx.ts` (the spark, its flame, the veil), `credits.ts`, `hits.ts`, `dash.ts`, `loft/layout.ts` and `loft/home.ts`.
- The parts, one folder a world:
  - `loft/`: `set.ts`, the room; `sneak*.ts`, LOFT-A; `stove*.ts`, `hearth.ts` and `cat.ts`, LOFT-B.
  - `glass/`: `glassworks.ts` and `glass-*.ts`.
  - `regatta/`: `balloons.ts` and `balloons-*.ts`.
  - `railway/`: `night.ts`, the shared night; `express*.ts`; `fireworks*.ts`.
- `apps/rube/checks/spark.ts`, run by `npm run check:shows`, holds the picker names, the recording and its YouTube cue, the world order, the doors on their phrases, the dash on the roll's strokes, no jump inside a world (0.04 cells a millisecond), every door a match cut on the screen (1% of the frame a millisecond, all but at the camera's own cuts), the camera cutting inside a world only on `CAMERA_CUTS`, each on an eighth of the theme, with the cutaway to the cat under 1.5 s, the spark on its wick at the start and from the first last chord on, every strike on the music (±35 ms of an onset or ±30 ms of a grid eighth), every heavy coda chord and both last chords struck, nothing in the silence, the spark in frame under Zoom (all but in the cutaway to the cat), never hidden more than 2.5 s, no firework breaking on the moon (against the show's own camera), one ball, and the credits' words.
- The share card is `public/shows/mountain-king/opus55-spark.png`, the trestle crossing at 116.4 s.

## How to run it

- `npx vite --port 8971 --strictPort`, then open `/shows/mountain-king/opus55-spark/`.
- `npm run check:shows` for the show's checks; `npm run build` runs every check and the site build.
- The card: `npm run cards` with a dev server up redraws every take's card. Nothing else needs regenerating: the onsets file is measured once, and the mp3 is the recording as downloaded (`scripts/shows/mountain-king-cue.sh`).
