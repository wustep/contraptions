# La La Land, Seb's

Copyrighted recordings. They play only from YouTube (the label's own uploads); no copy of the audio is in the repo. Nothing here claims any right to it. Attribution: `apps/rube/src/shows/versions/la-la-land/SEBS_ATTRIBUTION.txt`.

Open it at `/shows/la-la-land/opus5-5/`. In the Shows picker it is **Opus 5.5**, a take of the work **Epilogue**.

## What it is

A Rube Goldberg machine plays Justin Hurwitz's *Epilogue* from La La Land (2016), whole, 7:34 to its last chord. Then the room is silent while Mia goes to the door, and *The End* comes in under the nod, the band and the credits. 510 s in all.

The Epilogue is the film's "what if": Sebastian, at the piano in his own club five years on, sees Mia in the audience with her husband. As he plays their theme, the film replays their story the way it might have gone. The show follows it scene by scene. Every scene is a machine, and every machine strikes the music.

**The cast**
- **Sebastian is the blue ball** (`#4C7FD9`). He is the thread: his path is the chain of parts, and he makes things go.
- **Mia is the yellow ball** (`#F2C230`). She is company in every scene. She gets her life from timing: a hesitation, a catch-up, a turn toward him, a bow.
- **David is the grey ball** (`#8E8579`), her husband, his outline warm with their table's candle. He is only in the real club, at the start and the end, never in the dream.
- **Their son is the small green ball** (`#72AE5E`, yellow and blue together). He is only in the home movie.

**The frame.** La La Land's first image is a narrow old frame stretching out to CinemaScope. Here the room as it is plays in the old narrow frame (the Academy's 1.37 to 1, pillarboxed in the 16 to 9), and the dream is wide: the masking at the sides opens as the dream begins on Lipton's (41.03 to 43), closes in again as the dream drains at the waking (451.5 to 453.73), and opens once more when the band's stage blazes (478.05 to 480.2). Once the dream has opened wide, the picture never goes to black until he wakes: it changes its scene the way a stage does, with a curtain, white, a cloth flown in or out, a door past the lens, or white light, and the only two darks it keeps are the trumpet's iris and the night between the stars and the home movie, where one star stays lit (`lens.ts`, `transitions.ts`).

**The other road.** Once, the way it really went shows through: at her show (113.5 to 115.5), in the lead-in bar while the house holds its breath, the near-empty house of the film's real night flickers through, the seats empty and the house gone grey behind him (not him: in grey he would read as David). On the downbeat he springs up and the full house is back as it rises.

Every piece is new, and so is every place. Nothing is drawn from Machine's worlds, from the other takes, or from Liftoff. What is shared with Liftoff is the kit's pattern (parts built to timed slots, company balls, an authored camera) and the Shows infra it added (see *How it is built*).

## The music

**The audio.**
- The show plays the label's "Justin Hurwitz - Topic" uploads from YouTube, as two embeds on one clock: the Epilogue whole (`_vpCaKQXhMg`), then The End (`PMbrnvyLTdg`) from 464 s (`sebs/index.ts`). No copy of the audio is in the repo, and `check:shows` holds that the take has no local file.
- The clock was measured once from a mix of the same two uploads (`scripts/shows/sebs-mix.sh`: the Epilogue from its first sample, untouched, then The End from 464.0 s; no gain, fades or timing changes), which is not kept.

**Its clock.** `scripts/shows/sebs-onsets.py` measured the mix once (numpy and ffmpeg) into `scripts/shows/plans/sebs-onsets.json`:
- every onset (1,980), with its strength and, where the texture is thin enough, the top voice's pitch (the piano's melody, the trumpet's solo);
- two steady stretches as combs: the dream's 128.01 bpm from the kiss to the end of the Hollywood number (downbeats on k ≡ 1 mod 4; strong beats sit a median 5 ms off it), and the Paris club's 122.83 bpm;
- the film's landmarks, each snapped to its onset (the hush 61.777, the kiss 65.515, the burst 143.639, the jazz 214.877, the knocks 264.719 and 268.655, the dip 336.62, the last chord 453.73, the band 478.052), found by lining the recording up with the picture;
- loudness every half second.

The rest of the cue is rubato, and a strike there lands on a measured onset.

## How it keeps time, and what `check:shows` holds it to

Each part is handed a slot (the show time the ball arrives, the time it must leave) and builds its lane out of timed waypoints, so a strike lands where the music is by construction. `apps/rube/checks/sebs.ts`, run by `npm run check:shows`, holds:
- the chrome (Opus 5.5 under Epilogue, no byline), the mix, its length and its credit;

- 567 strikes, every one within 30 ms of a measured onset or a comb beat, and every part strikes;

## How it is built

- **The version file:** `apps/rube/src/shows/versions/la-la-land/opus5-5.show.ts`. Everything with weight is behind `load()`.
- **The show:** `.../la-la-land/sebs/`.
  - `lens.ts`: the frame (`aperture`, and the `masking` drawn over everything, covers too: the room narrow, the dream wide); the lens the whole picture is seen through (a little fall-off at the corners), but for the home movie, which has its own; and the grade: the room as it is muted and colder, the dream in full colour, with the colour coming in at Lipton's, draining at the waking, coming back first to his stage when the band comes in, in a pocket that grows as the camera draws back, and everywhere on The End's swell.
  - `piano-figure.ts`: the gold piano the planetarium draws among the stars, and over the city at the end.
  - `transitions.ts`: the covers the stage changes place under (the spotlight's iris, the curtain, white, dark, the door, the iris).
  - `physics.ts`: gravity and hops. `index.ts`: the performance (the camera, the covers, the credits, the YouTube soundtrack).
  - `kit.ts`: the part contract (`Slot`, `Built`, `Company`), timed `route` and `carried` lanes, `lay()` with seam checks, and drawing helpers (`frame`, `glow`, `beam`, `ring`).
  - `show.ts`: a `Show` with eleven universes on one clock, and the company merged in.
  - `score.ts`: the order, the seams, the covers, the end's pull-back.
  - `camera.ts`: the director, a monotone cubic through the parts' keys.
  - `music.ts`: the clock read from the onset file. `hits.ts`: every strike, gathered.
  - `worlds.ts`: the cast and each place's palette. `city.ts`: the city of stars. `street.ts`: the club's block and its street. `credits.ts`: the end credits.
  - The parts, one folder a place: `club/` (the piano, the room, its people, the opening, the finale; `geometry.ts` fixes the piano's numbers), `liptons/`, `theatre/`, `studio/`, `audition/`, `paris/`, `night/`, `movie/`.
- **Shared infra**, byte for byte the same as Liftoff's (#88), so either can land first:
  - `Framing.angle` and the roll in `drawWorld`, which this take does not use;
  - `Performance.titles` and the page's words layer, for the credits;
  - `ShowVersion.director`, for the byline (since removed: the picker has no bylines);
  - the styles for both.
- **Measuring the clock again.** `sh scripts/shows/sebs-mix.sh` rebuilds the measuring mix from the two uploads, and `python3 scripts/shows/sebs-onsets.py` measures it. Neither needs to run unless the recordings change.
