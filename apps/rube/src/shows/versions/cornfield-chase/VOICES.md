# Cornfield Chase — Voices

Private tech demo. The recording is Hans Zimmer's *Cornfield Chase* (Interstellar, 2014), used only here. It is not cleared for a public Shows release. Credit stays in the panel.

Open `/shows/?show=cornfield-chase&take=voices`. Zoom is the frame this take was composed for: both lanes, and the targets ahead of the ball, sit inside it.

## The clip

The show's zero is 70.146s into the recording, and the show runs 48s, to about 118s. That is the build into the loudest part of the chase, where the piano and the low end are both present. The file ends while the music is still full, just before the recording drops away near two minutes.

## The clock

Both voices use one pulse of 0.176s, the period that lines up with the piano band across this window. It is a shared grid, not a transcription of every note.

Horizontal progress is the same function of that clock for both: 2.5 cells a second. The hero ball's lane owns x. The bass ghost reads x back off that lane, including on the hops, so a hit in the bass is the same distance down the run as the arpeggio note it stacks under.

| Show time | Recording | Arpeggio | Bass |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–11.3s | 70.1–81.4s | Every other pulse | Every 8 pulses |
| 11.3–31s | 81.4–101s | Every pulse | Every 6 pulses |
| 31–48s | 101–118s | Every pulse | Every 4 pulses, disk and anvil only |

The arpeggio is a six-note rise and a drop back toward its rail. The note on the downbeat is the larger one, and it is where the bass strikes, so the accent is a vertical stack.

## What is drawn

- **Ahead of the hit.** An arpeggio target is an open disk for 0.7s before the ball. A bass target — portal, disk, or anvil — is an open silhouette for 1.45s, with ticks while it wakes. It fills when the actor arrives.
- **Where the hit was.** Arpeggio ink fades over 0.85s. Bass ink is a stamp plus a slower ring, gone by 1.7s. This is separate from the few frames of motion trail the engine already draws behind the hero.
- **The bass actor** is the existing ghost ball, drawn by this take, riding the lower rail and lifting into each mouth.

Outlines on the bass are heavier than the stock stroke, so the silhouettes still read under Zoom.
