# Nostos

`/shows/odysseus/opus55/` · **Nostos**, one take, **Opus 5.5**, on the Movies shelf.

**Nostos · after The Odyssey.** Ludwig Göransson's *Odysseus* is track 20 of *The Odyssey (Original Motion Picture
Soundtrack)* (Back Lot Music, 2026). In Christopher Nolan's film it is the cue under Odysseus's confession to
Penelope and the second, hellish sack of Troy. Here it is played whole by YouTube from Back Lot Music's own upload
(WCxx_OVOVUw), from its first sample, and then the end credits run on into the quiet after it: 549 s in all. No
recording is shipped (`ATTRIBUTION.txt`). The research is in `docs/shows/nostos/research.md` and the cue's measured
structure in `docs/shows/nostos/cue.md`.

*Nostos* is the Greek for a homecoming, the word the *Odyssey* is about.

## The idea

Nolan reads the poem as a reckoning. His Odysseus is a veteran who cannot face what he made at Troy ("His ultimate
mistake is the horse"), the gods appear only as weather, and the ending is exile west. So the show tells the whole
film in order, as one machine along one sea, cut away below the waterline like a tank, so that what sinks is seen
going down.

It opens on Nolan's own first image, the black horse half-sunk in the surf. It sets out home that same night: Ithaca,
Penelope's loom, the suitors, Telemachus, Argos, the bow and the twelve axes, all of them placed before the hero leaves
Troy. Then it goes the long way back through the trials to them. Every trial is a contraption the ball runs, and every
strike is on the music.

**The cast is locked.** Everyone who is a person is a ball:
- Odysseus is a bronze ball, for the age that ends with him.
- His men are clay balls. There are fourteen at Troy, fewer after every trial, and none after the storm.
- Penelope is a madder-red ball, the colour of her thread.
- Telemachus is a paler, smaller bronze ball.
- The suitors are slate balls. Antinous, their first, is darker and bigger.
- Calypso is sea-glass, only ever on Ogygia.
- At Troy, the Trojans are terracotta, Troy's own clay. Agamemnon is gold, as Mycenae's death masks are, and bigger
  than any man. His host is the same clay as Odysseus's men.

Everything else is drawn: the horse, Athena's statue, the Cyclops, the giants, the dead, the Sirens, Apollo's cattle,
Argos, the loom and the bow. No god is ever seen. Zeus is the bolt and Poseidon is the sea.

**One job per prop:**
- The loom keeps time: Penelope weaves by day and unpicks by night, through the prologue and the seven years.
- The gate's gears let the Greeks into Troy.
- The stake on its fulcrum blinds the Cyclops.
- The block and the line bind Odysseus to the mast.
- The fishermen's hoist brings him up Ithaca's cliff.
- The bow is the trial.

## How it is made

`odysseus/nostos/`:
- `show.ts` is one world on one clock (`NostosShow`): the balls of the cast, each on a path of its own. The stage draws
  what the show lists (`ShowPoint.balls`), so the hero is never drawn by the thread's default, and his mark turns only
  as he rolls (`path.ts`: rolls, true-gravity flights, rides).
- `scene.ts` and `voyage.ts` lay the voyage out as scenes, each owning a window of show time. A scene says where the
  hero, the men, the ships and the camera are in its window and what is drawn there, and hands them to the next at a
  measured seam (`SEAMS`), so nothing steps at a seam. `score.ts` lays them end to end.
- `camera.ts` is an authored director: monotone cubics through holds and follows; cuts only on the music; dissolves for
  the cross-cuts.
- `world/` is the sea (a cutaway whose swell is the score's weather), the sky (keyed light along the voyage,
  `world/light.ts`), the black ship, and depth layers for far things.
- `scenes/` holds the places. `ithaca/` is the set the show begins and ends in: the island, the hoist, the palace cut
  open, the loom, the bow's crank, the axes, Argos, the skiff and the cast's choreography.

The look is flat fills and few ink lines. Ink weights scale with the camera, like the balls' own outlines. Depth comes
from layers paling toward the air, and light only from where it comes from, as flat shapes on what it falls on. There
is no glow, bloom or sparkle, and nothing is written on the frame. The end credits are the page's (`credits.ts`),
painted into the frame only in a saved video.

## The clock

The cue keeps a steady beat only through its middle, so `scripts/shows/odysseus-onsets.py` measures:
- its stretches and landmarks;
- every onset, with its strength;
- the one comb, 0.7501 s from 188.35 to the cut, each beat moved onto its own attack.

The result is `scripts/shows/plans/odysseus-onsets.json`. `apps/rube/checks/nostos.ts` holds:
- every strike the story turns on to that file (the heaves, the spears, the host going in, the boulder, the stake, the
  pulse, the bolt, Argos, the shuttle's passes, the twelve axes, the steps, the tries, the bow, the loose, the arrow
  taking Antinous, the meeting);
- every bare camera cut to it;
- the hero, his family, his men and the suitors to never jumping, coming or going in shot except across a dissolve
  (and the one place a fist closes over two men);
- the credits and the soundtrack to what they owe.
